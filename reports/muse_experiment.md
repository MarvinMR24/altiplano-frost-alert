## What was sent to Muse

B1, anomaly review (corrections):
- The EDA anomaly list has 43160 flags. 38667 of them fall on an hour that a feature reads (at or before 18:00 local of the night that uses it).
- Tiny negative de-accumulation values in precipitation and shortwave radiation are a known ERA5 artifact. They were clipped to 0 in code and never sent:
- tp_mm: 38321 hourly values clipped to 0
- ssrd_wm2: 802 hourly values clipped to 0
- 277 remaining flagged hours can change a feature in the train and validation years (test-year flags are never sent). All are hourly jumps; there are no stuck values and no dew point above temperature.
- Sample: 277 records (cap 300; stratified by field and rule, seed 42), 14 calls of up to 20 records.
- Each record: an opaque id, the field, unit, flag, the flagged value and the hourly values of the same variable plus t2m and d2m at relative offsets h-3 to h+3, cut at the 18:00 local snapshot of the night that uses the value. No dates, years, hours of day, cell names, coordinates, elevation, labels or frost flags.

B2, feature proposals (one call): the allowlisted feature names (without latitude and elevation, which identify cells), their units, a one-line meaning and train-only mean, std, p5 and p95. No rows, labels, dates or places. The statistics of `prev_night_frost` imply the training frost rate of the previous night; no per-row label is sent.

Rationales:

- cooling_extrap_6h_c: Extrapolates late-afternoon cooling trend six hours to approximate midnight temperature.
- soil_air_excess_c: Warmer soil than air stores daytime heat that slows nocturnal radiative cooling.
- dry_clear_index: Dry air combined with clear afternoon amplifies nocturnal radiative heat loss.
- calm_dry_index: Calm winds below 3 m/s limit mixing while dry air enhances radiative cooling.
- clear_sky_persist: Sustained clear skies from afternoon to evening maximize overnight longwave loss.
- lw_drop_afternoon_evening: Falling longwave from afternoon to evening signals clearing skies and stronger cooling.

## Limitations

- Labels come from the same ERA5-Land reanalysis as the features, not from stations.
- There is little real anomaly material: tiny negative de-accumulation values and hourly jumps, mostly in dew point and temperature during morning warming. There are no stuck values and no dew point above temperature. B1 can only change a small number of feature values.
- B bundles B1 and B2, so this experiment cannot say which part helped or hurt.
- Rows outside the sample are unchanged by Muse (only clipped). Test-year flags were not reviewed, so on test B differs from A only by clipping and the B2 features.
- Labels are never edited: a corrected morning temperature changes the previous-night feature of the next night but not the label of the night it belongs to.
- Muse-generated per-row columns (`daily_summary`, `frost_likelihood`) are NOT part of B: they would require sending every row of the history, which the project rules forbid. Future work: a capped, masked sample with a separate condition and its own budget.
- Prices used for cost are configured in `configs/muse.yaml` and marked verify. Token counts come from the API usage field.
- Run history: 14 review calls and 1 proposal calls in the usage log returned invalid JSON. A first real attempt used max_tokens 4000; Muse Spark counts reasoning tokens toward that limit, so every answer was cut (finish_reason length). Those calls are logged and included in the cost; their requests differ from the current ones, so they are not reused.
